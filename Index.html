Est. 2016, Ashgrove Valley

A one-room studio, a wood kiln, and four firings a year.

Ashgrove Pottery is run by a small team of three throwers out of a converted barn at the edge of Ashgrove Valley. We make what a wheel and a wood kiln allow — nothing is cast, nothing is mass-produced, and every batch sells out before the next one is thrown.

How a piece is made

From clay to kiln

01

Wedging & centering

We source stoneware clay from a supplier two valleys over, wedge it by hand to clear air pockets, then center it on the wheel — the step every thrower spends their first year learning.

02

Throwing

Each form is pulled up by hand. Because nothing is molded, sibling pieces in a batch are close in size but never identical — you're buying the wheel's own variation, not a flaw.

03

Trimming & drying

Once leather-hard, a piece is trimmed to its final profile, then left to air-dry slowly for one to two weeks before its first firing.

04

Glazing

We mix three house glazes in-house — wood ash, celadon, and a rust oxide — and dip each piece by hand, batch by batch, in small buckets we test before every firing.

05

Wood firing

The kiln is loaded and fired with wood four times a year. It runs for eighteen hours and cools for two full days before we ever open the door.
